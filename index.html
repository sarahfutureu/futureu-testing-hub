Understanding the Tests (PSAT, SAT, ACT, AP) ▼

Standardized tests are ONE piece of college applications—not the only piece, but one that many colleges use to compare students from different schools and backgrounds.

PSAT 8/9 (Usually 9th Grade)

  • Practice test for the PSAT/NMSQT and SAT
  • Tests reading, writing, and math skills
  • Scores don't count for anything - pure practice
  • Gives you baseline to see where you're starting

PSAT/NMSQT (Usually 10th & 11th Grade)

  • Practice test for the SAT
  • Junior year: qualifies for National Merit Scholarships (top 1%)
  • Usually taken in October
  • Scores are practice - not sent to colleges

SAT (Usually 11th & 12th Grade)

  • College admissions test, now primarily digital
  • Sections: Reading & Writing, Math (each given in two adaptive modules)
  • Scored out of 1600 (200–800 per section)
  • Calculator allowed on all math questions
  • Offered multiple times per year nationally

ACT (Usually 11th & 12th Grade)

  • College admissions test (alternative to SAT)
  • Tests: English, Math, Reading, Science, optional Writing
  • Scored out of 36 (composite of four sections)
  • Offered 7 times per year

AP Exams (Any Year Taking AP Classes)

  • Tests for specific AP courses
  • Scored 1-5 (3+ typically earns college credit)
  • Taken in May after completing AP course
  • Different from SAT/ACT - tests specific course content
CRITICAL TRUTH: Your test scores are ONE piece of college applications. Many colleges are now test-optional. Your GPA, activities, essays, and recommendations also matter. Test scores are not a measure of your intelligence or worth.

📅 When to Test: Timeline by Grade

9th Grade: Introduction Year (Low Pressure) ▼

Focus: Building strong academic skills, light test awareness

Fall

PSAT 8/9 (if your school offers it) - pure practice, no stakes

Year-round

Focus on doing well in your classes (this IS test prep)

Maybe

Take one practice SAT or ACT at home just to see format (optional)

9TH GRADE: WHAT YOU DON'T NEED TO DO
  • Don't start serious test prep
  • Don't take SAT or ACT officially
  • Don't stress about scores
  • Don't compare yourself to others
  • Just build foundation and awareness
10th Grade: Planning & Light Prep Year ▼

Focus: PSAT/NMSQT in October, planning for junior year testing

August-September

Learn about PSAT, light review if desired

October

Take PSAT/NMSQT (practice for SAT, National Merit consideration)

November

Receive PSAT scores, understand what they mean

December-January

Learn about SAT vs ACT, decide which to take

Spring

Maybe take practice SAT and ACT to see which feels better

Spring/Summer

Light prep or diagnostic tests if desired

10TH GRADE: MAIN GOALS
  • Take PSAT/NMSQT in October
  • Understand test formats and what's expected
  • Decide on SAT vs ACT (or both)
  • Build strong math and reading skills in classes
  • Maybe start very light prep late in year
11th Grade: Peak Testing Year ⭐ ▼

Focus: Official SAT/ACT testing, multiple attempts, solid scores before senior year

August

Some students take first official SAT or ACT

August-September

Start serious test prep (3-6 months before first test)

October

PSAT/NMSQT (last chance for National Merit)

Fall/Winter

First official SAT or ACT attempt (Dec, March common)

Spring

Second SAT or ACT attempt (April, May, June)

May

AP Exams if taking AP classes

Summer

Possible third attempt if needed

11TH GRADE: MAIN GOALS
  • Take SAT or ACT 2-3 times to get your best score
  • Ideally have solid scores by end of junior year
  • Take PSAT/NMSQT in October
  • Take AP exams in May if in AP classes
  • Balance testing with schoolwork, activities, and mental health

Your test plan should fit around your life, not replace it. Scores matter, but so do sleep, mood, and not hating junior year.

12th Grade: Final Attempts & AP Focus ▼

Focus: Final SAT/ACT if needed, AP exams, then closure on testing

August-September

Final SAT/ACT attempt if needed

October

Last SAT for Early Decision/Early Action deadlines

November

Last SAT for most Regular Decision deadlines

December

Final SAT/ACT opportunity for Regular Decision

January-April

Testing season is OVER, focus on applications and decisions

May

AP Exams (final push)

12TH GRADE: MAIN GOALS
  • Final retakes in Aug-Dec if needed
  • After that: STOP testing, focus on applications
  • Take AP exams in May
  • Remember: test scores are submitted, move on to other parts of applications

⚖️ SAT vs ACT: Which Test Should You Take?

The short answer: they're very similar and colleges accept both equally.

SAT Overview

  • Time: About 2 hours 14 minutes
  • Sections: Reading & Writing, Math
  • Score: Out of 1600
  • Pacing: Slightly more time per question
  • Math: Calculator allowed on all questions
  • Style: More reasoning-based, can feel "trickier"

ACT Overview

  • Time: 2 hours 55 minutes (without Writing)
  • Sections: English, Math, Reading, Science
  • Score: Out of 36
  • Pacing: Faster pace, less time per question
  • Math: Calculator allowed throughout
  • Style: More straightforward, tests knowledge directly
How to Decide Which Test to Take ▼

Here's the best way to choose:

  1. Take a full-length practice test of BOTH (free online)
  2. Score both tests
  3. Compare:
    • Which score is higher relative to that test's scale?
    • Which test FELT better to you?
    • Did you run out of time on one more than the other?
    • Did one test format match your strengths better?
  4. Choose the one where you scored better OR felt more comfortable
  5. You can also take both if you want (some students do)
IMPORTANT NOTES
  • Colleges accept SAT and ACT equally - there's no "better" test
  • You don't have to decide in 9th or 10th grade
  • Take practice tests of both before committing
  • Most students focus on one test, but taking both is fine
  • Your PSAT score can give you insight into SAT performance
