1. Understanding the Tests

Overview: What Tests Exist and Why

Standardized tests are ONE piece of college applications. Not the only piece, but one that many colleges use to compare students from different schools and backgrounds. They are also a common source of stress for teens, so part of this hub’s job is to protect your mental health while you navigate them.

Main Tests You’ll Encounter

PSAT 8/9 (Usually 9th Grade)

  • Practice test for the PSAT/NMSQT and SAT.
  • Tests reading, writing, and math skills.
  • Scores don't count for anything – pure practice.
  • Gives you a baseline to see where you're starting.
  • Many schools offer this in fall of 9th grade.

PSAT/NMSQT (Usually 10th & 11th Grade)

  • Practice test for the SAT.
  • Junior year: qualifies for National Merit Scholarships (top 1% of scorers).
  • Tests reading, writing, and math.
  • Usually taken in October.
  • Scores are practice – not sent to colleges.
  • Sophomore year: good practice, no scholarship opportunity.

SAT (Usually 11th & 12th Grade)

  • College admissions test, now primarily digital; tests reading, writing, and math and is scored out of 1600.
  • Sections: Reading & Writing, Math (each given in two adaptive modules).
  • Scored out of 1600 (200–800 Reading & Writing, 200–800 Math).
  • A calculator is allowed on all math questions (including a built-in graphing calculator in the digital test).
  • Offered multiple times per year nationally.
  • Some schools offer SAT School Day (during school hours in spring).
  • Scores are sent to colleges as part of applications.

ACT (Usually 11th & 12th Grade)

  • College admissions test (alternative to SAT).
  • Tests: English, Math, Reading, Science, optional Writing.
  • Scored out of 36 (composite of four sections).
  • Offered 7 times per year nationally (Sept, Oct, Dec, Feb, April, June, July).
  • Some schools offer ACT in-school testing.
  • Scores sent to colleges as part of applications.

AP Exams (Any Year Taking AP Classes)

  • Tests for specific AP courses (AP Biology, AP US History, etc.).
  • Scored 1–5 (3+ typically earns college credit).
  • Taken in May after completing AP course.
  • Different from SAT/ACT – these test specific course content.
  • Optional but recommended if taking AP classes.

Critical Truth

  • Your test scores are ONE piece of college applications.
  • Many colleges are now test-optional (you choose whether to submit) – test-optional does not always mean “test-blind.”
  • Your GPA, activities, essays, and recommendations also matter.
  • Test scores are not a measure of your intelligence or worth.
  • Most students take SAT or ACT multiple times and improve.

2. When to Test: Timeline by Grade

Different tests happen at different points in high school. Here's what's typical for each grade:

9th Grade: Introduction Year (Low Pressure)

  • Focus: Building strong academic skills, light test awareness.
  • Fall: PSAT 8/9 (if your school offers it) – pure practice, no stakes.
  • Year‑round: Focus on doing well in your classes (this IS test prep).
  • Maybe: Take one practice SAT or ACT at home just to see format (optional).

9th Grade: What You Don’t Need to Do

  • Don't start serious test prep.
  • Don't take SAT or ACT officially.
  • Don't stress about scores.
  • Don't compare yourself to others.
  • Just build foundation and awareness.

10th Grade: Planning & Light Prep Year

  • Focus: PSAT/NMSQT in October, planning for junior year testing.
  • Aug–Sept: Learn about PSAT, light review if desired.
  • October: Take PSAT/NMSQT (practice for SAT, National Merit consideration).
  • Nov: Receive PSAT scores, understand what they mean.
  • Dec–Jan: Learn about SAT vs ACT, decide which to take.
  • Spring: Maybe take practice SAT and ACT to see which feels better.
  • Spring/Summer: Light prep or diagnostic tests if desired.

10th Grade: Main Goals

  • Take PSAT/NMSQT in October.
  • Understand test formats and what's expected.
  • Decide on SAT vs ACT (or both).
  • Build strong math and reading skills in classes.
  • Maybe start very light prep late in year.

11th Grade: Peak Testing Year

  • Focus: Official SAT/ACT testing, multiple attempts, solid scores before senior year.
  • August: Some students take first official SAT or ACT.
  • Aug–Sept: Start serious test prep (3–6 months before first test).
  • October: PSAT/NMSQT (last chance for National Merit).
  • Fall/Winter: First official SAT or ACT attempt (Dec, March common).
  • Spring: Second SAT or ACT attempt (April, May, June).
  • May: AP Exams if taking AP classes.
  • Summer after 11th: Possible third attempt if needed.

11th Grade: Main Goals

  • Take SAT or ACT 2–3 times to get your best score.
  • Ideally have solid scores by end of junior year.
  • Take PSAT/NMSQT in October.
  • Take AP exams in May if in AP classes.
  • Balance testing with schoolwork, activities, and mental health.

12th Grade: Final Attempts & AP Focus

  • Focus: Final SAT/ACT if needed, AP exams, then closure on testing.
  • Aug: Final SAT attempt if needed.
  • Sept: Final ACT attempt if needed.
  • Oct: Last SAT for Early Decision/Early Action deadlines.
  • Nov: Last SAT for most Regular Decision deadlines.
  • Dec: Final SAT/ACT opportunity for Regular Decision.
  • Jan–Apr: Testing season is OVER, focus on applications and decisions.
  • May: AP Exams (final push).

12th Grade: Main Goals

  • Final retakes in Aug–Dec if needed.
  • After that: STOP testing, focus on applications.
  • Take AP exams in May.

Overall Timeline Summary

  • 9th: Awareness and foundation building.
  • 10th: PSAT/NMSQT + planning for SAT/ACT.
  • 11th: PEAK testing year – get your scores.
  • 12th: Final attempts early, then DONE with SAT/ACT.
